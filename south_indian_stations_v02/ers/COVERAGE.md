## Pointwork and clearance review
Shared-node topology alone was not accepted as sufficient. Physical refinement replaces duplicate lead-rail sections with tapered blades, trims duplicate sleeper/fastener batches inside turnout envelopes, introduces common extended bearers and shared ballast, and replaces rail intersections with local casting polygons separated by two 45 mm normal flange channels. Check-rail centre inset 100 mm yields 45 mm clear space for 60/50 mm heads. Simple representative node 1008213472 is at local(563.817,37.329), joining ways 1453154556/285881585/49015817; its physical crossing is near(549.85,39.75). Complex junctions remain visual reconstructions rather than exact manufacturer/interlocking layouts. Common bearers use station-axis spacing, not a certified turnout tie schedule.

All OHE mast candidates are tested against every retained track centreline; supports require at least 2.35 m centre clearance and avoid bridge/stair walkways. Alternative-side placements are tried; a local mast is omitted if no safe candidate is found. Explicit stems and links attach support equipment to the wire system. These are gross geometry-clearance tests, not railway loading-gauge certification.

### Final running-rail method supersedes the local crossing patches
The completed running heads, webs, feet and tapered blades use a global polygon union with ALL route flange channels subtracted. This supersedes both the early full-width rail-cut prototype and the intermediate local-casting-envelope method. The earlier methods were rejected during physical review, not retained as finished geometry. All obsolete rail/casting/nose overlays are removed by the final loader. The source modules are retained to calculate support/bearer/check-rail context, while `prepare_rail_solids.py` and `geometry/rail_solids.json` define the finished running surfaces.
